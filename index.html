3 · Connect: Relate it to our course

Choose at least two of the four connections below. Use the sentence starters and course vocabulary to make your explanation precise.

A. Colonial society: diverse but unequal

Connect the images of slavery to our documentary and lessons. Colonial society included enslaved African people, Indigenous peoples, women, indentured servants and poor labourers, but rights and power were very unequal.

Starter: “This connects to our lesson on colonial society because … The society was diverse, but …”

B. Change and continuity

Connect the book to colonisation, slavery, emancipation, Reconstruction, segregation and the Civil Rights Movement. Explain why abolition was a major advance but not the end of inequality.

Starter: “The cartoon links … to … This shows historical change because … However, …”

C. Progress: real but incomplete

Does the cartoon communicate the idea that progress is “hard-fought, hard-won, incomplete, but always possible”? Does it show incomplete as clearly as progress?

Starter: “The cartoon illustrates the idea that progress is … However, it gives less attention to …”

D. Metaphors and simplification

Like the melting pot, salad bowl and mosaic, this cartoon simplifies a complex society. What does it highlight, and what does it leave out?

Starter: “Like the metaphors we studied, the cartoon highlights … but does not fully show …”
